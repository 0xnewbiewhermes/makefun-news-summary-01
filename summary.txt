Russia and Ukraine agreed in principle to a large prisoner-of-war exchange as two days of US-mediated talks ended in Abu Dhabi, though territory remained unresolved, Reuters and AP reported. The US Justice Department charged three Iran-backed operatives over nearly 20 plots in the US and Europe, including an attempted attack on Britain's RAF Fairford, while Secretary of State Marco Rubio said major combat operations against Iran were no longer needed, the New York Times reported. British counterterrorism police arrested a 41-year-old US Army soldier assigned to a NATO unit on suspicion of spying for Russia, Reuters reported. At the COP31 summit in Antalya, UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres told some 102 heads of state and government that slow climate action had put the world on a path to destruction, Just Security reported. Hoax bomb threats sent to about 6,300 Italian schools forced hundreds of evacuations, with similar emails reaching schools in Ireland and Scotland, CNN reported. Israel's new parliament was sworn in after a deadlocked election left Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu without a majority, the BBC reported, while Gaza health officials said Israeli strikes killed at least seven people. In Washington, the House voted 211-204 to shelve impeachment articles against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, and US consumer prices rose 0.4 percent in October, lifting expectations of a December rate increase. US stocks rallied as CoreWeave earnings lifted AI shares, with the S&P 500 closing up 1.31 percent, AP reported. Spain held its first general strike since 2012, and Portugal faced flooding from Storm Charles, according to Reuters. The Philippines confirmed Myanmar junta leader Min Aung Hlaing is barred from next week's ASEAN summit in Manila, the Philippine News Agency reported. US measles cases passed 5,100 this year, the most since 1991, the CDC said.
